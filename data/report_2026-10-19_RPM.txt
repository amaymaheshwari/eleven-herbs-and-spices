## SECTION 3: CUSTOMER SEGMENTS AND JOBS

**Fact.** RPM serves three broad customer categories: (1) professional contractors and tradespeople, (2) industrial and commercial end-users, and (3) do-it-yourself (DIY) consumers through retail channels. Per RPM's FY2024 10-K, no single customer accounted for more than 10% of consolidated net sales.

|---------------|---------------------|------------|----------------|

| Industrial/commercial users | ~25% | Carboline, Stonhard, Ruscoe | HIGH — safety-critical applications |
| DIY/Consumer (retail) | ~25% | Rust-Oleum, Varathane, DAP | LOW-MEDIUM — brand loyalty + shelf position |

> **Note:** Revenue mix estimated from segment disclosures in FY2024 10-K and earnings call color (Q4 FY2024 earnings call, July 25, 2024). Exact customer-type breakdown is not publicly disclosed.

**Analysis.** The professional/contractor and industrial customer bases are RPM's moat core. Switching costs for Carboline industrial protective coatings or Tremco roofing systems are very high because:
1. Products are specified by engineers in project drawings
2. Applicators must be trained and certified
3. Failure (e.g., a coating delaminating on a chemical plant) carries enormous liability

**Fact.** Tremco's roofing systems require certified applicators through its "Tremco Roofing & Building Maintenance" program. This creates a proprietary applicator network that competes incumbently on renewal projects.

**Analysis.** The DIY/Consumer segment has lower switching costs. Rust-Oleum commands meaningful brand equity and shelf placement at Home Depot and Lowe's, but consumers can and do switch to store brands or Krylon (Sherwin-Williams). The key procurement blocker in Consumer is **retail shelf space**, which is controlled by category buyers at the two major home improvement retailers.

**Inference.** RPM's dominant risk in Consumer is losing incremental shelf space at Home Depot or Lowe's, not losing consumers directly. This is a B2B relationship risk that requires monitoring.

### Do-Nothing Prevalence

**Analysis.** In CPG and PCG, "do-nothing" (deferred maintenance) is common during budget-constrained periods but creates pent-up demand — every year of deferred maintenance on a roof or industrial coating increases the eventual spend. This creates a cyclically lagged but durable demand floor.

## SECTION 19: M&A STRATEGY AND OPTIONALITY

**Fact.** RPM has a long history of bolt-on acquisitions — the company made 170+ acquisitions between 1993 and 2019, averaging 6–8 per year. Largest deal: GCP Applied Technologies acquired in January 2022 for approximately $2.7 billion (enterprise value), financed with debt.

**Fact.** GCP acquired for ~2.7B at approximately 15× EBITDA (GCP's FY2021 EBITDA was ~$180M). Integration into CPG segment was largely complete by FY2024, with GCP contributing positively to CPG margins. Management cited $150M+ in synergies from GCP over the first two years.

**Analysis.** Post-GCP, RPM's deal pace has slowed — the leverage incurred ($2.7B acquisition) required balance sheet repair before resuming M&A. Net debt/EBITDA of ~2.1× provides room for $500M–1.0B in bolt-on transactions before approaching 2.5× leverage.

**Build-Buy-Partner Framework:**
- **Build (organic):** Preferred for product innovation (new formulations, new SKUs); 3–5 year payback
- **Buy (M&A):** Preferred for channel access, geographic expansion, and technology gaps (e.g., digital monitoring for roofing); disciplined target multiples of 7–9× EBITDA for bolt-ons
- **Partner:** Limited use; Tremco WTI contractor relationships function as a loose partnership model

**Capability Gaps That Cannot Be Built Organically in Time:**
1. **Digital asset monitoring / predictive maintenance for roofing** — could accelerate via acquisition of a small PropTech (property technology) firm (~$50–150M)
2. **India/Southeast Asia distribution for industrial coatings** — faster via joint venture or acquisition than greenfield
3. **Concrete repair specialty in Europe** — Sika's scale makes organic catch-up slow; bolt-on in European construction chemicals could accelerate

**Regulatory Environment:** U.S. antitrust environment for industrial deals under a Biden-to-Trump administration transition has become more permissive for industrial/materials deals in 2025 (per antitrust commentary from Allen & Overy, January 2025). RPM's fragmented target universe (small/mid specialty chemical companies) is unlikely to draw antitrust scrutiny.
