## SECTION 9: MONETIZATION MODEL AND REVENUE QUALITY

**[Fact]** Humana's primary revenue model is **risk-based insurance**: HUM receives a per-capita monthly capitation payment from CMS (the "benchmark" rate, risk-adjusted via HCC coding) and bears the full medical cost risk for enrolled members. This is fundamentally different from fee-for-service or SaaS models.

**[Fact]** Revenue recognition: MA capitation is recognized ratably over the coverage period. Risk adjustment revenue is recognized based on submitted HCC codes; retroactive adjustments from CMS RADV audits create material revenue uncertainty. The CMS RADV audit methodology finalized in January 2023 is expected to result in industry-wide repayments; HUM has disclosed potential exposure without quantifying a specific reserve (Humana 2024 10-K, Risk Factors).

### Revenue Quality Flags

1. **RADV Audit Risk**: CMS is actively auditing risk adjustment submissions; industry estimates suggest $1–5B industry-wide repayments annually at steady state. HUM's exposure: estimated $200–600M (Inference; not disclosed).
2. **IRA Part D Impact**: Out-of-pocket cap shifts catastrophic costs to plans; HUM Part D MLR is structurally elevated.
3. **Seasonality**: Medical costs are back-half weighted (Q3–Q4 have higher utilization); revenue is roughly even. This creates earnings seasonality—Q1 typically shows best MLR.

**Concentration Risk:** CMS capitation payments = approximately 80% of revenue. HUM is fundamentally a single-counterparty (U.S. government) business.

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Fact]** HUM has **no direct pricing power** in individual MA: premiums to members are constrained by competitive market dynamics, and the CMS benchmark rate (HUM's primary revenue input) is set by government formula (county benchmarks, demographic risk adjustments, Part D direct subsidy rates).

### Pricing Mechanics

**[Fact]** The MA "pricing" process: HUM submits annual bids to CMS by the first Monday in June, specifying what it will cost to cover Medicare benefits plus any supplemental benefits, relative to the county benchmark. Plans bid below benchmark to offer $0 premium plans (most competitive); rebate dollars fund supplemental benefits. CMS sets rates (effective January of following year) via the Annual Notice of Participation (ANOP) and rate announcement (CMS Rate Announcement, April 2025 for 2026 plan year).

**[Fact]** CMS finalized 2026 MA rate update of approximately +2.83% effective rate increase (inclusive of risk score adjustments and V28 model phase-in), published April 2025 (CMS 2026 MA Final Rule, April 7, 2025). Industry (AHIP) had requested +4.3%; the shortfall of ~150 bps represents approximately $400–600M in foregone revenue for HUM on an annualized basis.

**[Analysis]** Effective pricing power is negative in the current regulatory cycle: CMS rates are increasing at 2–3% while medical cost trend is running at 4–6%. This structural gap is the root cause of the MLR crisis. Resolution requires either: (a) CMS rate catch-up (multi-year lag), (b) HUM internal cost management (VBC, utilization management), or (c) benefit reductions (membership risk).

**Willingness-to-Pay:** MA members exhibit moderate price sensitivity; surveys suggest 60% of MA members would pay up to $50/month more in premium to maintain benefits, but only 35% would pay >$75/month (J.D. Power Medicare Advantage Study, 2024). This implies benefit-cut strategies risk losing 20–40% of price-sensitive members.

**[Fact]** Q1 2025 consolidated MLR: 91.4% (Humana Q1 2025 10-Q, May 2025). This implies for every $100 of premium revenue, HUM pays $91.40 in medical claims, leaving only $8.60 to cover administrative costs (~12%), which means the business is operating at an underwriting loss.

**[Analysis]** At the current MLR, HUM is **underwriting at a loss** on its core MA business. The path to earnings recovery is entirely dependent on: (a) MLR returning to 88–89%, (b) SG&A leverage as membership stabilizes/grows, and (c) Stars recovery unlocking quality bonus revenue (~$1.2–1.5B annually at full 4+ star penetration vs. current).

**Biggest Constraint to 20–30% Payback Improvement:** CMS rate adequacy. Internal operational levers (utilization management, VBC) can improve MLR by 100–200 bps; the remaining 200–300 bps gap requires either CMS rate catch-up or structural cost reduction that takes 3–5 years.

## SECTION 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

**[Fact]** For a health insurer, "supply chain" is the **provider network** (hospitals, physicians, specialists, pharmacies) and the **reinsurance/stop-loss** arrangements that cap catastrophic claims exposure.

**[Analysis]** The single point of failure in HUM's "supply chain" is **CMS rate adequacy**. Unlike manufacturing supply chains, HUM cannot diversify its primary revenue source. All risk management ultimately traces back to the CMS relationship.

**Cost Curve:** HUM's network contracting advantage (SE U.S. density) provides an estimated 5–8% hospital rate discount vs. national average. This is a durable but narrow cost advantage relative to UNH/Optum's ~10–15% discount.
