## Textron Aviation

**Fact:** Textron Aviation produces Cessna Citation business jets, Cessna turboprops (Caravan, Denali), Beechcraft King Air turboprops, Beechcraft piston trainers, and Hawker business jets. It also provides aftermarket parts and services.

**Analysis:** King Air remains the dominant twin-turboprop globally with ~50% market share. Citation jets compete in the light-to-midsize jet segment. The Cessna Denali (single-engine turboprop) program represents an important new entrant against Pilatus PC-12 — a key roadmap item.

**Inference:** Denali certification delays (FAA certification ongoing as of 2025) represent a near-term roadmap risk. Each 6-month delay equals approximately $150–200M of deferred revenue based on reported backlog.

## Textron Systems

**Fact:** Products include Shadow UAS (Unmanned Aerial Systems), Aerosonde, Piston Bully (ground vehicles), RIPSAW unmanned ground vehicles, and the TCTS (Tactical Common Data Link Communications Station).

**Analysis:** Systems is the highest-growth optionality segment but also most dependent on Pentagon discretionary spending and program-by-program wins.

## Kautex (Industrial)

**Fact:** Kautex manufactures plastic fuel systems and fluid management systems for automotive OEMs globally. It is the world's largest producer of plastic fuel tanks.

**Inference:** Kautex faces a structural revenue decline as OEMs electrify powertrain lineups. EV fuel tank content = ~zero vs. ~$150–200/vehicle for ICE. At ~40M units/year ICE production declining 3–5%/year, Kautex's addressable production base shrinks meaningfully. We model Kautex revenue declining 3–5% annually 2025–2029.

**Hardest-to-copy capability:** Bell's tiltrotor technology (V-280) represents a 50-year engineering heritage starting with the XV-15 program. No competitor has a production-ready alternative — this is the irreplaceable asset.

**Technical debt limiting scale:** Kautex legacy manufacturing footprint (Europe-heavy) creates fixed cost absorption risk as volumes decline.

**Analysis:** The aftermarket ecosystem is the most important moat-deepening mechanism. **Ecosystem-mediated revenue share:** Aftermarket is ~19% of total Textron revenue (~$2.0B) — this is high-quality, recurring revenue.

**Fact:** Textron Aviation uses a direct sales force (N. America) and dealer network (international) of ~100 authorized dealers in 70+ countries.

**Analysis:** The biggest funnel bottleneck is OEM production slot allocation — not demand generation. With backlogs of 18–24 months for popular models (King Air 360, Citation CJ3+), the constraint is production, not sales.

**What doubling pipeline without doubling OpEx requires:** Increasing production throughput via lean manufacturing and supplier development — already underway at Wichita facilities (Textron Aviation's primary manufacturing base, Kansas).

Automotive OEM direct sales, long-term supply agreements (3–5 years), global footprint in Germany, U.S., China, Brazil. GTM is irrelevant to investment thesis given structural headwinds.
