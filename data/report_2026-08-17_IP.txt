## SECTION 6: ECOSYSTEM AND PLATFORM HEALTH

**[Analysis]** IP operates primarily as an **industrial manufacturer**, not a platform business. Ecosystem dynamics are relevant but limited compared to technology companies. Key ecosystem considerations:

### Fiber Supply Ecosystem
**[Fact]** IP owns approximately 296,000 acres of forestland in the US (IP 10-K FY2024) — dramatically reduced from historical holdings following forestland divestitures. IP sources the majority of its fiber through long-term supply agreements with private timber owners and public forestlands.

- **Risk:** Fiber cost is 35–40% of cash cost of production; pine pulpwood prices in the US South rose 15–20% in 2022–2023 due to biomass energy competition (RISI Wood Fiber Review, 2024)
- **Mitigation:** IP's supply agreements and regional fiber basin relationships provide 60–70% supply security; remainder subject to spot market

### Recycled Fiber (OCC — Old Corrugated Container) Ecosystem
**[Fact]** DS Smith operates a primarily recycled fiber-based system in Europe (OCC ~70% of fiber input per DS Smith Annual Report 2023/24). OCC prices are highly volatile — US OCC prices ranged from $20/ton to $200/ton between 2019–2023 (RISI OCC Price Index).

### Partner & Customer Integration
- IP's Box-on-Demand systems installed at ~50+ major customer sites — creates operational lock-in
- DS Smith's "Circular Economy" partnerships with retailers (Tesco, Lidl, Amazon Europe) create preferred supplier status

### Minimum-Viable Ecosystem Health Metric
**[Inference]** OCC price sustainability < $100/ton for DS Smith European operations; pine pulpwood prices < $35/ton (US South basis) for IP North American operations

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Fact]** Containerboard pricing is set by oligopolistic market dynamics — IP, Smurfit WestRock, PCA, and a few others effectively set market prices through announced price increases, which are followed by the industry within 30–60 days.

### Historical Price Cycle

| Year | NA Linerboard Price (42-lb, $/ton) | IP EBITDA Margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | ~$565 | ~14% |
| 2021 | ~$720 (peak) | ~18% |
| 2022 | ~$800+ (super-cycle) | ~20% |
| 2023 | ~$625–650 (trough) | ~13% |
| 2024 | ~$650–680 | ~14–15% |
| 2025E | ~$700–730 (recovery) | ~16–17%E |

*[Fact] Prices sourced from RISI/Fastmarkets containerboard price indices; margins estimated from IP financial disclosures.*

### Pricing Power Assessment
**[Analysis]** IP has **moderate pricing power** — it is one of 2–3 price leaders in North America, but pricing is fundamentally determined by supply/demand balance, not by IP's unilateral decisions. In a tight market (utilization > 90%), IP can lead and sustain price increases. In a loose market, prices are set by the marginal cost of production.

**Willingness-to-Pay:** Corrugated packaging represents < 2% of end-product COGS for most customers, making them relatively price-insensitive to modest increases (<10%). At larger increases (>15–20%), customer pushback and substitution (lighter-weight board, recycled alternatives) increases.

### Price-Volume Elasticity
**[Analysis]** Based on historical cycles, IP's demand elasticity for containerboard:
- Price increase of $50/ton (+7%): Volume loss < 1% (inelastic)
- Price increase of $100/ton (+15%): Volume loss 3–5% (modest demand destruction)
- Price increase of $150/ton (+20%+): Volume loss 8–10%; customers switch to alternative suppliers or lighter board weights
