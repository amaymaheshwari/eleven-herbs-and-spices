### Workflow and Pain Points

**Analysis:** Central bank customers' primary "job-to-be-done" is ensuring every note in circulation is genuine and machine-readable. The cost of a failed authentication system is existential — it would undermine public trust in the currency. This creates near-zero price sensitivity for mission-critical authentication components.

**Switching Cost Quantification (Inference):** For a central bank to switch from CXT's authentication platform to a competitor (e.g., Giesecke+Devrient, De La Rue), it would require: (a) a 2–4 year qualification program, (b) replacement of installed sensor hardware across tens of thousands of ATMs and cash-sorting machines (estimated $50–200M+ for a major market), and (c) full re-training of maintenance staff. Net switching cost is estimated at >$200M for a G7-scale deployment, vs. incremental spend with CXT of ~$5–20M/year — making switching economically irrational in most scenarios.

### Procurement Blockers

**Fact:** Government procurement requires multi-year tender processes, often with security clearance requirements. The primary procurement blocker is the **specification process** — once CXT's authentication features are written into a nation's currency specification, competitors effectively cannot displace it without a full currency redesign (typically 7–10 year cycles).
