| Government/municipal | CPG | ~5% | Medium-High (spec-driven, price-inelastic) |

## Switching Costs

**Analysis.** Switching costs are highest in the CPG and PCG segments:
- Contractors become "certified applicators" for RPM brands (Tremco, Dryvit, Stonhard) — losing certification has real economic cost
- Industrial OEMs have qualification processes for coatings that take 6–18 months to transfer to a competitor
- Spec-writing by RPM's technical sales force into architect/engineer drawings creates quasi-lock-in on project-by-project basis

**CBG switching costs are low** — a consumer can switch from Rust-Oleum to a competitor brand easily. This segment is brand-equity and distribution-intensity driven.

## Do-Nothing Prevalence

**Inference.** Deferred maintenance (do-nothing) is a meaningful risk in residential CBG (~15–20% of potential purchases deferred in housing downturns) but low in CPG (regulatory and warranty obligations force spending).

## Procurement Blocker

**Analysis.** In CPG/PCG, the key blocker is specification approval by architects or facility managers — RPM's technical sales force and application-training programs are the primary unlock. In CBG, the blocker is shelf placement and consumer marketing ROI (RPM competes for shelf space against Sherwin-Williams, PPG, and Behr/Masco).

# SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

## Pricing Governance

**Fact.** RPM uses a decentralized pricing model — individual operating companies historically set pricing, with some corporate oversight post-MAP. MAP includes implementation of enterprise pricing analytics tools to rationalize pricing across segments.

## Elasticity Evidence

**Analysis.** RPM successfully passed through 8–12% cumulative price increases during FY2022–FY2023 raw material inflation cycle with limited volume loss in CPG/PCG (volume held within -1 to -3% during peak pricing). This demonstrates pricing power in professional segments. CBG showed higher elasticity — volume declined ~5–8% in FY2023 as consumers traded down or deferred, consistent with DIY segment norms.

## Willingness-to-Pay Research

**Inference.** Professional contractors' willingness-to-pay is primarily driven by performance guarantees and warranty value, not unit price. For a $10M commercial roof replacement, a $50,000 premium for a Tremco system with a 20-year NDL warranty is immaterial (<0.5% of project cost) — implying very low price elasticity in CPG's core market.

## Packaging Strategy

**Analysis.** CPG/PCG package as "system solutions" (primer + membrane + sealant + warranty) — this bundling makes price comparison vs. competitors difficult and increases average transaction size by 30–50% vs. selling components individually.

## ARPU Ceiling Before Churn Inflects

**Inference.** In CPG, no clear churn inflection identified below 15–20% price premium to alternatives (given switching costs). In CBG, churn begins inflecting at approximately +10–15% price premium to comparable store-brand products.
