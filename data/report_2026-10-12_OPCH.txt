**[Inference]** Do-nothing prevalence is LOW for established patients (high switching cost, clinical continuity) but HIGH for new patient starts (payer directs to whichever network is preferred). The key battleground is **new patient acquisition**, not retention.

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Analysis]** Pricing governance: OPCH negotiates rates with payers annually or biannually. Drug component pricing is largely pass-through (AWP [Average Wholesale Price] minus a discount, or MAC [Maximum Allowable Cost] pricing). Professional services rates are negotiated directly and carry more pricing power.

**[Fact]** Medicare reimbursement for home infusion services follows the Home Infusion Therapy (HIT) benefit established under the 21st Century Cures Act, with rates set by CMS annually. For 2025, CMS maintained home infusion professional services rates with a modest inflation adjustment (~2–3%) per the CMS Home Infusion Therapy Final Rule (November 2024).

**[Analysis]** Pricing power assessment:

| Segment | Pricing Power | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial professional services | Moderate | Annual renegotiation; OPCH's scale provides leverage |
| Medicare professional services | Low-Moderate | CMS-set; modest annual inflation adjustments |
| Biosimilar drug spread | High (near-term) | Manufacturer rebates + biosimilar discounts widen spread |
| Brand biologic spread | Low | Competition among infusion providers reduces margin |

**[Inference]** OPCH's true pricing power lies in **professional services rates** and **drug spread management** (buying drugs at a discount vs. AWP and billing at AWP). The biosimilar wave benefits OPCH because biosimilars carry larger discounts from manufacturers, widening the spread even as headline drug revenue per dose falls.

**ARPU ceiling:** We estimate average revenue per patient per year at approximately $18,000–$22,000 (implied from revenue/estimated patient count). Before churn inflects, we believe pricing can increase ~3–5% annually for chronic therapies — driven by therapy complexity inflation rather than price increases per se.

## SECTION 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

**[Fact]** OPCH's supply chain dependencies:

Loss of preferred purchasing status with a manufacturer would increase drug acquisition cost. We estimate 10–15% of drug spend is in programs where OPCH has preferred/exclusive distribution arrangements.

**[Analysis]** Supply chain risks:
- Drug shortage risk: Biologics manufacturing is complex; a shortage of a major therapy class would reduce OPCH volumes in that class. Historical drug shortage events (2019–2023) have affected IVIG supply; OPCH manages this with multi-supplier strategies.
- Distribution: OPCH's dispensing pharmacies are largely self-operated; the company is not dependent on third-party pharmacy fulfillment for most therapies.

**[Inference]** Single point of failure: IVIG supply disruption. IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin) is manufactured from human plasma; supply is inelastic in the short term (12–18 month production cycle). A major supply disruption would affect 15–20% of OPCH revenue. Mitigant: OPCH holds 30–60 days of IVIG inventory and has contracts with multiple plasma fractionators.
