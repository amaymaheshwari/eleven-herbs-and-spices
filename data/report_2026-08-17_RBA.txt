# SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

## 10A. Pricing Governance

*Fact.* RBA employs a dual-fee model: sellers pay a seller's commission (or flat listing fee) and buyers pay a buyer's premium. The buyer's premium on commercial equipment auctions is typically 10–15% of purchase price. Seller fees vary by contract but are typically 5–8% of GTV. Combined take rate is approximately 13–18% of GTV on commercial transactions. [Source: RBA SEC filings, investor presentations]

*Fact.* IAA charges insurance carriers a per-unit fee (not a percentage of GTV), which is supplemented by buyer premiums. IAA's total take per vehicle improved materially as online buyer competition increased GTV, making the carrier per-unit fee increasingly attractive relative to recovery value.

## 10B. Elasticity Evidence

*Analysis.* Sellers in distress (receiver/lender mandated sales, government surplus) have essentially zero price elasticity — they must sell and RBA's buyer liquidity is the value. Sellers with optionality (fleet managers, contractors) show moderate elasticity — they compare RBA take-rate to dealer offers. The implied GTV premium RBA delivers (estimated 8–15% vs. dealer) must exceed the take-rate to retain seller loyalty.

*Inference.* We estimate the ARPU ceiling before seller churn inflects is approximately a combined take-rate of 18–20% of GTV for commercial (vs. ~15% today), above which the GTV premium vs. dealer trade-in is no longer sufficient. IAA's carrier fee is contractually constrained and less exposed to elasticity, but is subject to renegotiation at renewal.

| **Regulatory complexity** | Low-Medium | Title processing, state-by-state salvage compliance | Moderate barrier to entry |

## 14B. Network Effect Analysis

*Analysis.* The commercial marketplace exhibits a **bilateral network effect**: more sellers attract more buyers (greater selection) and more buyers attract more sellers (higher price discovery). This is the classic marketplace dynamic. The effect is strongest at the auction-event level — an auction with 200 registered bidders will achieve materially higher GTV per lot than one with 20. RBA's scale advantage in buyer density is its primary moat.

*Inference.* The moat is **deepening**, not stagnant, as online buyer penetration grows. A buyer in Germany can now participate in a Texas equipment auction in real time — this was not possible pre-2015. Each year of online buyer growth widens the gap between RBA and regional competitors.

**Event Most Likely to Collapse the Moat Within Two Years:** A major insurance carrier (representing >10% of IAA volume) switching exclusively to Copart, combined with Copart launching a competitive commercial equipment marketplace using its yard network. Low probability (~10–15%) but high impact.
