# SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**Fact**: CSL management has discussed annual price increases on CCM products in virtually every earnings call from 2017 through 2024. In 2021–2022, price increases of 8–15% were implemented to offset raw material cost inflation (isocyanates, polyols, petrochemicals). Volume impact was reportedly minimal based on management commentary.

**Analysis**: CCM pricing power sources:
1. **Market share leadership**: With 35–40% commercial membrane share, CSL is effectively a price setter, not a price taker
2. **Warranty system lock-in**: Mid-project switching is economically irrational for a building owner
3. **Energy code tailwinds**: Cool-roof mandates in IECC (International Energy Conservation Code) 2021 and state-level codes increase demand for CSL's premium TPO products
4. **Competitor capacity discipline**: Firestone and Johns Manville have not materially added capacity, supporting oligopolistic pricing dynamics

**Pricing Elasticity Evidence**:
- 2022 price increases of 10–15% → CCM volume decline of only ~3–5% (Inference from management commentary on volume/price mix in 2022 10-K)
- 2023 price normalization with modest volume recovery → supports demand elasticity is low

**ARPU Ceiling Estimate**: Based on 2023–2024 CCM revenue per addressable commercial roof square, we estimate CSL has room to take 3–5% annual price increases before meaningful volume loss, provided competitors maintain pricing discipline (which the oligopoly structure supports). This implies a pricing ceiling approximately 25–30% above current levels before elasticity inflects—a ~5–7 year runway at current pace.

# SECTION 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

## CCM Supply Chain

| Input | Supplier Type | Dependency | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| MDI (Methylene Diphenyl Diisocyanate) | BASF, Huntsman, Covestro | Concentrated; 3–4 global producers | High; MDI price volatility directly impacts gross margin |
| Polyols | BASF, Dow, LyondellBasell | Moderate concentration | Moderate |
| Carbon Black, Plasticizers | Multiple | Less concentrated | Low-Moderate |
| Glass fiber, polyester | Multiple | Competitive market | Low |

**Fact**: CSL's CCM gross margins are significantly influenced by MDI prices, which are tied to the global isocyanate market (primarily driven by construction demand in China and automotive use). MDI price declines in 2023 provided gross margin tailwinds for CCM; any reversal would be a headwind.

**Single Point of Failure**: An extended MDI supply disruption (e.g., major BASF Verbund site outage) could constrain production. CSL maintains some inventory buffer, but a >30-day disruption would impact production meaningfully.

**Manufacturing Economics**: CCM is capital-efficient—CapEx requirements are modest relative to revenue (CapEx/Revenue ~3–4%). Depreciation/amortization provides a tax shield; ongoing maintenance CapEx is low. Growth CapEx for capacity expansion is occasional and well-telegraphed.

**CIT Supply Chain**: Wire, specialty insulation materials, and specialty alloys. More fragmented supplier base than CCM. Single-source risk exists for certain aerospace-grade materials but is managed through long-term supply agreements. Lead times for some aerospace materials can be 6–18 months—this was a constraint during the 2021–2022 aerospace recovery.
