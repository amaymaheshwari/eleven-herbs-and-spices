## SECTION 14: MOAT AND DATA ADVANTAGE

### Primary Moat: Regulated Franchise Monopoly
**Fact**: EXC's six utilities hold state-granted exclusive franchises for electricity and gas distribution in their defined service territories. These franchises are perpetual (subject to renewal processes that historically have never been revoked for investor-owned utilities in EXC's jurisdictions). The physical network — approximately $38B in Rate Base across ~140,000 miles of distribution lines and ~4,800 miles of transmission lines — constitutes an irreplicable asset. [EXC 2024 10-K; EEI Industry Data 2024]

### Secondary Moat: Regulatory Expertise and Relationships
**Analysis**: Managing six regulatory jurisdictions simultaneously requires deep institutional knowledge, legal expertise, and political relationships. This creates organizational barriers to entry and exit. EXC's regulatory affairs team — seasoned by decades of multi-state operations — represents a durable capability.

### Data Advantage
**Analysis**: AMI (Advanced Metering Infrastructure) deployment across EXC's territory (>80% of customers on smart meters as of 2024) generates high-frequency consumption data. This data supports:
- Demand forecasting and grid optimization
- DER integration and load management programs
- Regulatory filings and performance demonstrations

This data has competitive value in regulatory proceedings (demonstrating reliability improvements, justifying capex) but is not a revenue-generating data asset in the commercial sense.

### Moat-Deepening Evidence
**Fact**: EXC's transmission investment program ($10B over 5 years) is deepening the moat by increasing the physical asset base and expanding FERC-regulated revenue. Each dollar of approved transmission spend locks in a regulated return for 30+ years (asset life).

### Event Most Likely to Collapse the Moat
**Inference**: Illinois re-regulation risk — a legislative action creating a public/municipal utility or mandating state ownership of ComEd — represents the highest-impact, lowest-probability threat to the moat. Probability estimated at <5% over 24 months based on current Illinois political environment, but the ComEd scandal (2020–2022) raised this tail risk above historical norms.
