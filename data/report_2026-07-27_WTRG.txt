## SECTION 2: MARKET STRUCTURE AND SIZE

**[Fact]** The U.S. regulated water and wastewater utility market serves approximately 328 million people. The American Water Works Association (AWWA) estimated total water system infrastructure replacement needs at $1 trillion over 25 years as of 2023. The Environmental Protection Agency's (EPA's) 6th Drinking Water Infrastructure Needs Survey (2023) estimated $625 billion in drinking water infrastructure needs over 20 years.

**[Fact]** Regulated water utilities are a fragmented market. Per the National Association of Water Companies (NAWC) 2024 data, investor-owned utilities (IOUs) serve approximately 73 million people (~22% of U.S. population); government-owned systems serve the balance (~78%). IOUs hold ~15,000 community water systems but the top five IOUs—American Water Works (AWK), Essential Utilities (WTRG), California Water Service (CWT), SJW Group (SJW), and Middlesex Water (MSEX)—control the majority of IOU revenue.

### Market Sizing Table

| Segment | Total Addressable Market (TAM) | WTRG Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) | WTRG Current Revenue Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. Regulated Water/WW | ~$40B annual revenue (industry-wide) | ~$15B (IOU-addressable, 10-state footprint) | ~$1.7B (~11% of SAM) |
| U.S. Regulated Gas Distribution | ~$70B annual revenue (industry-wide) | ~$5B (PA/OH/KY/WV markets) | ~$1.0B (~20% of SAM) |
| Municipal Acquisition Pipeline | ~8,000 small municipal systems potentially privatizable | $3–5B over decade (management estimate) | Early innings |

**[Analysis]** Growth drivers:
- **PFAS regulation:** EPA's final Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) rule (April 2024) for PFAS compounds will require capital investment across all water systems; IOUs are better positioned to fund compliance than cash-strapped municipalities, accelerating privatization.
- **Lead Service Line (LSL) replacement:** EPA's Lead and Copper Rule Revision requires 100% LSL inventory and replacement within 10 years; utilities estimate $45–60B industry-wide cost.
- **Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA):** $55B for water infrastructure (2021–2026), with grants and low-interest loans available but also creating competitive dynamics.

**[Analysis]** Supply is the binding constraint, not demand. Water demand in WTRG's service territories is flat-to-slightly-declining per capita (conservation trends, efficient appliances) but system-level revenues are protected through decoupling mechanisms or volumetric adjusters in most states, meaning revenue is not directly at risk from conservation.

**[Inference]** The privatization pipeline (municipal-to-IOU conversions) represents the most significant TAM expansion vector, as it converts an ~8,000-system addressable universe into regulated rate base. WTRG has executed this playbook more aggressively than peers, completing over 275 acquisitions in its history.

## SECTION 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

**[Fact]** WTRG's critical supply chain inputs include:
- **Pipes and fittings:** Ductile iron, PVC, steel; primarily sourced domestically or from Canada/Mexico. Tariff exposure under 2025 trade policy changes could add 10–15% to pipe costs (Inference).
- **Treatment chemicals:** Chlorine, fluoride, coagulants. Primarily domestic suppliers; some global chemical supply dependencies.
- **Electrical power:** Water treatment is energy-intensive (~30–40% of water O&M). Power costs pass through in some jurisdictions but not all.
- **Construction contractors:** Third-party contractors for pipe replacement and system installation. Labor market tightness in 2022–2024 added 10–20% to construction costs per AWWA surveys.

**[Analysis]** Single point of failure: Over-reliance on third-party construction contractors in a tight labor market. WTRG has limited ability to self-perform large-scale infrastructure projects. If contractor costs rise materially above rate-case-embedded assumptions, earned ROE declines.

**[Fact]** 2025 Tariff exposure: The Trump administration's 2025 tariff regime (25% on steel/aluminum from most countries as of early 2025) directly increases the cost of ductile iron pipes, which are critical for water main replacement. Industry estimates suggest this could add $50–100M in incremental annual costs for a utility of WTRG's scale if not mitigated.

**[Analysis]** Mitigation: WTRG can seek recovery of increased material costs in future rate cases (prudency review required). However, there is typically a 12–24 month lag before cost recovery. In the near term, higher material costs pressure earned ROE.
