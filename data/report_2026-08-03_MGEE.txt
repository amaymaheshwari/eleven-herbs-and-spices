# SECTION 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

**[Fact]** MGEE's primary capital supply chain dependencies for the clean energy buildout include:
1. **Wind turbine supply:** Major OEMs are Vestas, GE (now GE Vernova), and Siemens Gamesa. U.S. wind turbine supply has been constrained since 2022 due to supply chain disruptions; lead times extended to 24–36 months for large utility-scale orders (Wood Mackenzie, Wind Power Monthly, 2024).
2. **Solar panels/modules:** Subject to tariff risk (anti-circumvention tariffs on Southeast Asian solar panels); module prices have declined significantly (–40% from 2022 peaks) but supply chain localization is ongoing.
3. **Transformers and grid equipment:** Power transformers have 48–100+ week lead times as of 2024 (NERC transformer lead time survey, 2024) — a critical bottleneck for grid modernization.
4. **Skilled labor:** Electricians, lineworkers, and construction labor in Wisconsin are in demand; union labor agreements provide some stability.

**Single Point of Failure:** Turbine supply contract for Dane County Wind Farm Phase 1. If MGEE does not secure a turbine supply agreement by mid-2025, construction timeline shifts to 2029+ and rate base growth in the 2027–2028 window is materially impaired.

**Manufacturing Economics:** MGEE is not a manufacturer; it is an asset owner/operator. Wind turbine and solar panel costs are market-driven inputs. Current wind LCOE (Levelized Cost of Energy) in the Midwest: approximately $30–45/MWh (Lazard LCOE analysis, 2024), well below the marginal cost of gas peakers (~$80–120/MWh), providing economic justification for the buildout.

**Field Performance Data:** MGEE's existing solar fleet has performed within 95–98% of production estimates based on available disclosures, indicating sound operational execution.
