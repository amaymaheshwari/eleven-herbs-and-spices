### Workflow and Mission-Criticality

**Analysis:**
- **Ratings**: Credit ratings are embedded in bond indentures, investment mandates, and regulatory frameworks (e.g., SEC Rule 17g-7, EU ESMA regulations). Switching cost = zero practical alternative for a rated entity; a downgrade has immediate market consequences. Mission-criticality = **extreme**.
- **Market Intelligence**: Portfolio analytics, risk management, and compliance workflows often embed MI data via API; switching requires recertification of data lineage and re-integration of systems. Switching cost = **high** (6–18 months of migration; estimated $0.5–2M per enterprise customer).
- **Commodity Insights**: Platts price benchmarks are the settlement references for CME Group energy futures. The Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) lists multiple contracts referencing Platts benchmarks; these cannot be switched without market-wide renegotiation. Switching cost = **extreme** for benchmark-dependent contracts.
- **Mobility**: VIN (Vehicle Identification Number)-level data is embedded in dealer management systems (DMS) and insurance telematics. Switching cost = **moderate** (3–9 months).
- **Indices**: Index methodology changes require regulatory/investor approval; passive fund sponsors face AUM risk if they switch. Switching cost = **high**.

### Do-Nothing Prevalence

**Analysis.** In Ratings, "do nothing" is not an option for issuers seeking market access — mandatory. In MI, the "do nothing" risk is moderate; smaller asset managers sometimes rely on Bloomberg terminals only (~35,000 terminal subscribers globally per Bloomberg estimates). In CI, do-nothing is low for energy traders relying on Platts for settlement.

### Procurement Blocker and Proof Required

**Analysis.** The primary procurement blocker in MI is **demonstrating differentiated analytics vs. Bloomberg Terminal**: customers must justify two enterprise contracts. Proof required: specific workflow coverage (e.g., private credit analytics, ESG data, M&A comparable analysis) where Bloomberg lacks depth. SPGI's Capital IQ Pro platform is the primary vehicle for this proof.

**Fact.** SPGI management stated in the Q4 2024 earnings call (February 12, 2025) that subscription price increases for Market Intelligence and Commodity Insights have averaged **5–7% annually** over the past three years, consistently above inflation.

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and LTV/CAC

## SECTION 14 — MOAT AND DATA ADVANTAGE

**Analysis.** SPGI's moat has four reinforcing layers:

### Layer 1: Regulatory Entrenchment (Ratings)
**Fact.** NRSROs are designated by the U.S. SEC under the Credit Rating Agency Reform Act of 2006. There are currently 10 registered NRSROs (SEC, 2024), but only three (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) are globally recognized as equivalent by major bond issuers, investors, and regulators in >100 jurisdictions. EU equivalence under ESMA's ECAI (External Credit Assessment Institution) framework further entrenches the Big Three. **Barrier:** Regulatory recognition requires decades of track record, methodology transparency, and global institutional acceptance — effectively irreproducible.

### Layer 2: Benchmark Incumbency (Platts / Indices)
**Fact.** The Platts crude oil Dated Brent assessment is the pricing reference for ~60–70% of globally traded crude oil (S&P Global Platts, 2023 methodology documentation). The S&P 500 Index is tracked by >$16T in passive AUM — a scale that makes alternative indices economically inferior (lower fund flows = higher tracking error = higher cost for ETF sponsors). **Barrier:** Network incumbency; competitive benchmarks require simultaneous adoption by a critical mass of market participants — a collective action problem.

### Layer 3: Data Depth and Exclusivity
**Analysis.** SPGI's proprietary datasets include:
- **Carfax VIN history database**: 40+ billion records (SPGI Mobility, 2024).
- **IHS Markit iBoxx bond pricing**: pricing coverage of >240,000 fixed-income instruments daily.
- **Platts editorial team**: Physical commodity market reporters embedded in spot markets globally — non-replicable without physical presence.
- **S&P Capital IQ financial database**: 135+ years of U.S. financial data; 200+ countries; used as the baseline for academic finance research (e.g., CRSP is the academic standard, but Capital IQ is the practitioner standard).

### Layer 4: Switching Costs and Embeddedness
**Analysis.** As detailed in Section 3, Platts and Indices have near-infinite switching costs for core users (legal/contractual embeddedness). Capital IQ is embedded in analyst workflows at >90% of major investment banks. Carfax is embedded in virtually every U.S. car dealership's sales workflow (Inference: Carfax was used in >7M vehicle transactions in 2023 per SPGI Mobility segment disclosure).

### Moat Deepening Evidence
- NRR >105% in subscription segments implies customers are buying more, not less.
- AI/Kensho tools being added to existing platforms at no incremental data cost → margin accretive.
- IHS Markit integration creates data cross-links (e.g., linking Platts energy price data to Capital IQ corporate financials) that neither legacy dataset offered alone.

### Event Most Likely to Collapse the Moat Within Two Years
**Inference.** The most credible moat-collapse risk is **regulatory disintermediation of the NRSRO framework** — specifically, a U.S. Congress mandate to move to "investor-pays" from "issuer-pays" for credit ratings (a proposal that has been discussed since 2008). This would structurally reshape rating economics. Probability over 24 months: ~3–5% (regulatory inertia is high; the issuer-pays model has been examined and retained multiple times since the Global Financial Crisis (GFC)).

## SECTION 15 — DATA AND AI ECONOMICS

### Data Sources, Ownership, and Exclusivity

**Fact:**
- **Platts assessments**: Proprietary, based on physical market transactions reported to SPGI editorial staff. SPGI owns the methodology and the published benchmark — exclusive.
- **Carfax VIN data**: Aggregated from state DMVs, auto manufacturers, and insurers under data-sharing agreements. SPGI/Carfax has the most comprehensive U.S. VIN database; Cox Automotive has ~70% overlap (Inference). Exclusivity is moderate.
- **iBoxx bond pricing**: Derived from dealer contributions and market sources; SPGI has term agreements with major broker-dealers. Moderately exclusive.
- **Capital IQ financials**: Aggregated from public filings (10-K, 10-Q), company-reported data. Not exclusive in content, but exclusive in **organization, normalization, and historical depth**.

### AI/Kensho Unit Economics

**Fact.** Kensho has built several commercial AI products:
- **Kensho SCRIBE**: Earnings call transcription and NLP; sold as add-on to Capital IQ.
- **Kensho NERD**: Named entity recognition for financial documents.
- **Kensho Classify**: Document classification for regulatory filings.
- **S&P Global Market Intelligence Chat**: Generative AI query interface (launched 2024).

**Analysis.** Marginal ROI of AI features vs. non-AI baseline (Inference):
- SCRIBE and NERD: Primarily internal cost-reduction tools (reduce manual data tagging labor); estimated savings ~$30–50M/year in staff costs.
- Generative AI Chat: Premium upsell feature; if ~10% of MI users pay $2,000/year premium → ~$100M incremental ARR. **This is not yet disclosed.**

### Vendor and IP Risk

**Analysis.** SPGI disclosed in 2024 10-K that it uses multiple cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) for data infrastructure. No single-vendor lock-in risk identified. LLM (Large Language Model) IP risk: SPGI uses both proprietary (Kensho) and third-party models (Anthropic, OpenAI). Risk that SPGI's data is used to train competitor models — mitigated by data access agreements and terms of service, but an emerging legal risk (see NYT vs. OpenAI litigation as precedent).

### Inference/Training Cost Trends

**Analysis.** SPGI's AI unit economics benefit from **pre-labeled, domain-specific proprietary data** — the highest-value input for financial LLMs. The cost to train a general-purpose LLM to match Kensho's financial entity recognition accuracy would require millions of labeled financial documents. SPGI's data moat is therefore **AI-defensive**: the proprietary data makes SPGI's AI outputs harder to replicate, even if the underlying model architecture is commoditized.
