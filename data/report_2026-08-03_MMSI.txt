**[Fact]** MMSI operates in a price-competitive but not purely commodity market. Key pricing dynamics:

**[Analysis]** Pricing governance is determined at three levels:
1. **GPO master agreements:** Multi-year contracts with annual price escalators (typically CPI-linked, 1–3%); caps on price increases; competitive bid processes every 3–5 years
2. **IDN (Integrated Delivery Network) agreements:** Direct contracts that may provide volume discounts of 5–15% vs. list price
3. **ASC and smaller accounts:** Closer to list price; less negotiating leverage by customer

**Elasticity Evidence:**
- Medical disposables demonstrate inelastic demand for clinically necessary products (hospitals cannot substitute away from hemostasis valves in cardiac catheterization procedures)
- Price sensitivity is higher for non-differentiated products (e.g., generic catheters) vs. proprietary designs (e.g., MMSI's Prelude hemostasis valve)
- **Inference:** MMSI's proprietary products command 10–20% price premiums vs. generic equivalents; commodity products face 1–3% annual price erosion

**Willingness-to-Pay Research:** Not publicly disclosed by MMSI. Industry norms suggest hospitals will pay a 5–15% premium for products that demonstrably reduce procedure time, complications, or total supply costs.

**ARPU Ceiling Before Churn Inflects:** In GPO-governed markets, the ceiling is effectively set by competitive alternatives. For proprietary products: MMSI has approximately 20–30% headroom above current pricing before losing contracts to competitors (Inference).

**Packaging Strategy:** MMSI bundles products into procedure-specific kits (e.g., cardiac catheterization trays), which increases average order value and switching costs.
