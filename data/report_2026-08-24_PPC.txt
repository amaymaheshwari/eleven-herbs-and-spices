**[Analysis]** Switching costs in chicken processing are moderate-to-low for commodity products (tenders, boneless breasts priced on the Urner Barry commodity index) and moderate-to-high for value-added/co-manufactured products where PPC has invested in customer-specific production lines, recipe development, and logistics customization.

**[Inference]** In periods of commodity price inflation, retail and foodservice customers may accept reduced promotional activity or delay product launches but do not exit chicken purchases — demand is non-discretionary at the menu/shelf level.

**[Analysis]** At foodservice, QSR menus have shown willingness to increase menu prices for chicken items (McDonald's McChicken, Chick-fil-A pricing increases in 2022–2023) without meaningful traffic loss, suggesting end-market elasticity is manageable.

**[Inference]** The practical ARPU ceiling before volume churn inflects is approximately $1.30–1.40/lb for boneless breast meat (where retail trade-down to pork or frozen alternatives begins). Current prices of ~$1.05–1.15/lb are below this ceiling, suggesting headroom.

# SECTION 14: MOAT AND DATA ADVANTAGE

## Moat Sources

**[Fact]** PPC's competitive advantages are structural rather than technological:

1. **Scale and network density:** 13+ processing complexes in the U.S., providing geographic coverage critical for national account supply agreements. Entry cost estimated at $3–5B to replicate.

2. **Grower relationships:** ~5,700 contract growers represent a captive supply network. Growers' barns (dedicated broiler houses) are purpose-built for PPC specifications — high switching cost for growers.

3. **Customer integration:** Multi-year supply agreements with QSR customers include co-developed product specifications, proprietary cut codes, and logistics integration. Switching cost for customer: 12–18 months of re-qualification.

4. **Brand equity (nascent):** Just BARE® brand growing in the premium/ABF space. Not yet a defensible brand moat but directionally positive.

## Moat Durability

**[Analysis]** The moat is moderate — rated 2.5/5 in our Quality Scorecard. It is defensible against new entrants (capital barriers) but vulnerable to:
- Tyson Foods aggressively taking market share via price competition
- Private-label retailers sourcing from international chicken (Brazil — BRF, JBS exports) when economically viable
- Regulatory changes to contract grower structures reducing the grower network advantage

## Moat-Collapse Event

**[Inference]** The event most likely to collapse the moat within two years: A sustained HPAI outbreak requiring mass depopulation of PPC's specific complex regions, forcing short-term supply disruption that allows competitors to capture PPC's customer relationships. Probability: 10–15% (based on USDA APHIS historical outbreak frequency and geographic concentration of PPC complexes).
