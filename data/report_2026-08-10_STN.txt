## SECTION 1 — THESIS FRAMING

**[Fact]** Stantec Inc. is a Canada-headquartered global professional services firm providing engineering, architecture, environmental science, project management, and consulting services, with fiscal year 2024 (FY2024) revenue of approximately CAD 6.7 billion (USD ~4.9 billion at average FY2024 CAD/USD ~0.735). **[Analysis]** The core value-creation thesis rests on Stantec's ability to compound Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) and Free Cash Flow (FCF) at rates that justify the current premium multiple (~18–20× EV/EBITDA forward) relative to engineering and construction (E&C) sector medians (~11–13×). This premium is predicated on Stantec being a "pure-play" consulting firm — not a contractor — with superior margin stability, high recurring revenue from long-duration public-sector frameworks, and a disciplined tuck-in Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A) strategy.

### Why-Now Catalyst (Dated)

**[Fact]** The U.S. Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) of November 2021 allocated USD 1.2 trillion over 10 years, with the bulk of design/engineering work flowing through in FY2024–FY2027 as projects reach engineering-notice-to-proceed stage. The U.S. EPA finalized PFAS drinking-water Maximum Contaminant Level (MCL) rules in April 2024 (effective June 2024), requiring utilities to comply by 2029, triggering an estimated USD 9–15 billion in treatment-plant engineering and remediation spend.

**[Analysis]** The 2025–2026 window represents the intersection of: (a) peak IIJA engineering-service demand, (b) PFAS compliance engineering ramp, and (c) Stantec's post-acquisition integration of Hydrology & Geomorphology (H&G) and other FY2023–2024 bolt-ons. This convergence creates a 24-month earnings-acceleration window that is the "why now" of the thesis — but the stock appears to have already partially priced this.

**[Analysis]** Consensus views Stantec as a premium compounder with 10–12% EPS growth baked in, trading at ~20× forward EV/EBITDA. The variant bear perspective (our emphasis given our risk-first mandate) is that: (1) U.S. political uncertainty around infrastructure appropriations (debt ceiling, DOGE-driven discretionary cuts) is underweighted by the Street; (2) CAD/USD foreign exchange (FX) risk is material given ~35% of costs are in Canadian dollars while revenue is increasingly U.S.-dollar-denominated (which is actually a tailwind at current CAD weakness, but creates reporting volatility for USD investors); (3) the premium multiple (18–20× EV/EBITDA) is vulnerable to re-rating if organic growth decelerates even modestly.

**[Inference]** The Street is pricing ~9% organic growth and further margin expansion with relatively high confidence. We assign lower probability (~45% vs. consensus implied ~65%) to the bull case materializing fully, creating a skew asymmetry that keeps us on the sidelines at current prices.

## SECTION 10 — PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Fact]** Professional services pricing in the public sector is governed by:
1. **Qualifications-based selection (QBS):** U.S. federal and most state governments select A&E firms on qualifications, then negotiate fees — creating a cost-plus rather than price-competitive environment (Brooks Act, 1972, governing U.S. federal A&E procurement)
2. **Framework rate cards:** UK and Canadian public frameworks set hourly rates updated annually, typically CPI-linked

**[Analysis]** This regulatory structure creates **structural pricing power for qualified firms.** Stantec's ability to raise rates is gated by: (1) its classification tier in government rate schedules; (2) competitive pressure from other pre-qualified firms.

### Historical Pricing Evidence

**[Fact]** Stantec management has guided to 3–5% annual billing-rate increases on framework agreements in FY2024, broadly in line with CPI + 1–2%. In the post-COVID labor inflation environment (FY2022–FY2024), the firm was able to pass through above-CPI rate increases given tight engineering labor markets.

### Pricing Governance

- Annual rate card reviews on frameworks
- Project-by-project negotiation on new lump-sum bids
- No published elasticity studies (private knowledge within firm)
