### Switching Costs & Pain Points
*Analysis:* MA switching costs are moderate at the individual consumer level — members can switch plans annually during open enrollment (Oct 15–Dec 7) and the Special Enrollment Period (Jan 1–Mar 31). However, **clinical continuity** (established PCP relationships, ongoing care coordination) and **pharmacy network lock-in** (preferred network pharmacies, formulary familiarity) create meaningful inertia. CenterWell-integrated members who receive primary care, pharmacy, AND insurance from HUM face much higher switching costs — this is the strategic logic of the integrated model.

### Procurement Blockers
*Fact:* The key purchase-unlock metric for individual MA is the annual "Medicare & You" handbook comparison and independent broker recommendations. Approximately 60–65% of MA enrollment occurs through licensed independent agents and brokers (FMOs — Field Marketing Organizations). HUM's broker relationships and commission rates relative to competitors are a key competitive variable. [AHIP, 2024 distribution data]

### Do-Nothing Prevalence
*Fact:* MA plan auto-renewal rates are approximately 70–80% annually (members who do not actively switch). This high inertia supports retention but also means that a member lost is hard to recover.

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

### Pricing Governance
*Fact:* HUM does not set its own premium rates freely. CMS sets county-level benchmark rates; HUM bids a premium against that benchmark and members pay zero additional premium if HUM bids at or below benchmark. MA plan pricing is therefore a function of: (a) CMS benchmark rates, (b) HUM's actuarial cost estimates, (c) competitive positioning in each market.

### Price-Elasticity Evidence
*Analysis:* The 2025 enrollment cycle demonstrated meaningful price/benefit elasticity. HUM reduced supplemental benefits by an estimated ~$500–$800 per member equivalent value in 2025 bids (to reduce losses) and subsequently lost ~500K–700K members during open enrollment — approximately 9–12% of its base. This implies a price-elasticity coefficient of approximately −0.8 to −1.2 for supplemental benefits in MA (1% benefit reduction → ~0.8–1.2% membership decline). This is a critical data point: **HUM cannot easily reduce benefits without losing members.**

### Monetization Change Log

| Year | Change | Impact |
|------|--------|--------|
| 2023 | Expanded supplemental benefits aggressively | +~600K members but MLR deteriorated |
| 2024 | Maintained elevated benefits despite rising costs | MLR ~92–93%, EPS guidance cut 3× |
| 2025 | Reduced supplemental benefits, repriced bids | Membership −500K to −700K |
| 2026 (planned) | Actuarially sound repricing + CMS rate tailwind | Expected membership stabilization |

### ARPU Ceiling Before Churn Inflects
*Inference:* Based on the above elasticity data, HUM can realistically recover ~100–150 bps of MLR via benefit design adjustments before member attrition accelerates beyond replacement rates. Full MLR recovery to ≤88% requires both benefit repricing AND medical cost management.
