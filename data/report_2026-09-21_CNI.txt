## Section 1: Thesis Framing

**[Fact]** Canadian National Railway operates 18,600 route-miles across Canada and the United States, connecting the Port of Prince Rupert (BC), Port of Halifax (NS), and the Port of New Orleans/Mobile (Gulf of Mexico) — the only North American railroad with tri-coastal reach (CNI 2024 Annual Report, filed March 2025).

**[Analysis]** The value-creation hurdle: CNI must grow revenue at 4–6% CAGR, compress its Operating Ratio (OR) from the FY2024 level of ~64.0% toward 59–61% by FY2027, and convert incremental revenue to FCF at a 35–40% incremental FCF margin. Failure to achieve OR improvement is the primary variant risk.

## Section 4: Product and Roadmap

**[Fact]** CN operates as a vertically integrated railroad: it owns and maintains its track, locomotives (~2,000 units), and railcars (~70,000 owned/leased), and employs its own crews (CNI 2024 Annual Report).

### Core Service Lines

**[Analysis]** CN's "hardest-to-copy" capability is its **Prince Rupert Intermodal Gateway** — a deep-water, uncongested port with a direct 85-hour rail corridor to Chicago. This corridor is 44 hours faster than LA/Long Beach to Chicago by ship+rail (Prince Rupert Port Authority, 2024 Annual Report). No competitor can replicate this geography.

**PSR 2.0 / "Falcon" Operating Plan:**
- **[Fact]** CEO Tracy Robinson launched the "Falcon" plan in 2023, targeting train-speed improvement to 26+ mph (vs. ~24 mph in 2022), dwell-time reduction to <8 hours (vs. ~9.5 hours in 2022), and locomotive productivity improvement of 15% by end-2025 (CN Q3 2024 earnings call, October 2024).
- **[Analysis]** As of Q1 2025, CN reported train speed of ~25.0 mph and dwell time of ~8.5 hours — progress but not yet at target. Full achievement would imply ~C$400–600M in annual cost savings (freight car cycle improvement + fuel efficiency).

**Technical Debt / Capital Constraint:** CN's average fleet age is ~15 years for locomotives. The transition to Tier 4 (lower-emission) locomotives requires ~C$500–700M in incremental capex over 2025–2028.

**[Analysis]** The single biggest "funnel bottleneck" is customer volume commitment: industrial customers delaying capex (mine expansions, new plant construction) directly reduces long-haul contracted volumes. CN's sales team cannot accelerate customer capex decisions.

## Section 10: Pricing Power and Elasticity Testing

**[Analysis]** CN's pricing power is a function of corridor monopoly position, fuel surcharge pass-through mechanisms, and long-term contract escalators.

**[Fact]** CN's fuel surcharge is tied to the Canadian National Energy Board weekly diesel price index; it adjusts weekly and represented approximately 8–10% of total revenue in FY2023–2024 (CNI Annual Reports 2023, 2024). This is a near-perfect pass-through mechanism, insulating CN from fuel cost inflation.

### Pricing Power by Segment

| Segment | Pricing Authority | Evidence of Elasticity | ARPU Ceiling Risk |
|---------|------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Captive bulk (grain, potash, coal) | Moderate — MRE/regulation caps | Low elasticity; no alternative | MRE statutory cap |
| Intermodal (competitive) | Low-Medium | High elasticity vs. truck | Truck rate parity |
| Petroleum (tank car) | High on captive corridors | Low — pipeline is slow/regulated alternative | Very high |
| Metals/Minerals | Medium | Medium — some truck competition | Regional monopoly |

**[Analysis]** CN's average revenue per RTM has grown at approximately 3–5% annually over FY2018–2024, consistently above North American CPI, demonstrating durable pricing power on its captive network. Intermodal yield is more volatile and truck-rate correlated.

**Packaging Strategy:** CN does not publish standard tariff schedules for most industrial freight; rates are privately negotiated, providing flexibility to price by customer value rather than published tariff, enhancing margin stability.

## Section 14: Moat and Data Advantage

**[Analysis]** CN possesses what we classify as a **Tier-1 infrastructure moat** — the highest durability category in our framework. The moat rests on four interlocking pillars:

### Moat Components

| Pillar | Evidence | Durability |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Network geography** | 18,600 miles; only tri-coastal NA railroad; government would not permit new entry | Permanent |
| **Sunk capital barrier** | ~C$14B replacement cost of track/bridges alone (our estimate; industry rule of thumb: ~$1M/route-mile) | Permanent |
| **Regulatory protection** | STB/CTA licensing; Class I designation; environmental review for new entry would take 20+ years | Near-permanent |
| **Customer switching costs** | Captive origin/destination infrastructure (grain elevators, mine sidings); industry-specific loading equipment | High |
| **Operating know-how (PSR)** | Network optimization algorithms, crew deployment models, locomotive utilization algorithms | Medium (replicable over 5–10 years) |

**[Fact]** The replacement cost of CN's network is estimated at US$35–45 billion by Morningstar equity research (April 2025), compared to CN's enterprise value of ~US$56B (at ~US$102/share). This represents an Enterprise Value (EV)/Replacement-cost ratio of ~1.3–1.6×, a typical range for high-quality regulated infrastructure.

**Moat-Collapse Scenario:** The most likely moat-weakening event is **STB-mandated reciprocal switching** on US corridors. The STB's 2022 final rule (effective 2024) allows short-line/regional railroads to request reciprocal switching access; a broad expansion to Class I interchange corridors could reduce CN's pricing on ~15–20% of US revenue. We estimate a worst-case scenario of ~C$250–400M annual EBIT impact — material but not moat-destroying.
