## Section 1: THESIS FRAMING

### Value-Creation Hurdle

**Fact:** AutoZone must generate sufficient free cash flow (FCF) and deploy it via share repurchases (the dominant capital-return vehicle) to grow intrinsic value per share faster than the S&P 500 plus 300 basis points (bps), while maintaining quality-of-earnings standards and protecting against a structurally levered balance sheet.

### Thesis Pillars (If-Then Conditions)

**Pillar 1 — Aging Vehicle Fleet Drives Durable DIY/DIFM Demand**
- *If* the U.S. vehicle population continues aging (average age ~12.6 years per S&P Global Mobility, 2024), *then* part-failure rates and repair frequency justify mid-single-digit SSS growth through FY2027.

**Pillar 2 — Mexico/International Expansion Creates Incremental Growth at Attractive Returns**
- *If* AutoZone sustains ~80–100 new store openings per year in Mexico and Brazil, *then* international EBIT contribution grows from ~15% of total to ~20%+ by FY2027, supporting a revenue CAGR of ~7–9%.

**Pillar 3 — Share Repurchases Compound Per-Share Value**
- *If* AutoZone repurchases ~$3.0–3.5B in stock annually at sub-intrinsic-value prices, *then* diluted share count declines ~8–10% over 24 months, generating ~8–10% EPS growth from buybacks alone, even on flat earnings.

**Pillar 4 — Commercial (DIFM) Expansion Extends the TAM**
- *If* AutoZone grows its Do-It-For-Me (DIFM) commercial sales at 8–12% annually, accelerating from ~$5.2B segment revenue (FY2024 estimate), *then* AutoZone narrows the gap with O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY) and Genuine Parts (GPC) in the higher-value commercial channel.

**Pillar 5 — Operational Leverage and Cost Discipline Protect Margins**
- *If* SG&A leverage is maintained (target: SG&A as % of sales flat-to-declining), *then* EBIT margins hold in the 19–21% range, sustaining FCF conversion above 85%.

## Section 3: CUSTOMER SEGMENTS AND JOBS

### Customer Mix

**Fact:** AutoZone serves two primary customer segments:

| Segment | % of U.S. Revenue (est.) | Customer Type | Job-to-Be-Done |
|---------|--------------------------|---------------|----------------|
| DIY (Do-It-Yourself) | ~75% | Individual consumers | Self-repair vehicles; save labor costs |
| DIFM / Commercial | ~25% | Independent repair shops, dealerships, fleets | Same-day/JIT parts delivery to complete repair jobs |

**Fact:** Per AZO FY2024 10-K, AutoZone does not break out exact revenue by segment but discloses domestic vs. international. The ~75%/25% DIY/DIFM split is an industry consensus estimate (Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs research; confirmed by management commentary in earnings calls) and is approximate.

### DIY Customer Profile

**Analysis:**
- **Demographics:** Predominantly male (70%+), aged 25–55, median household income $40,000–$75,000 (SEMA 2023 consumer research).
- **Pain Points:** High shop labor costs ($120–180/hour), vehicle downtime, and trust deficit with repair shops.
- **Mission-Criticality:** Vehicle reliability is high-stakes; incorrect part selection has safety implications. AutoZone's "ZNet" diagnostic system and trained store staff address this.
- **Switching Costs (Moderate):** Low financial switching costs between AutoZone, O'Reilly, and Advance. However, **behavioral loyalty** is driven by store proximity (AutoZone has 6,300+ U.S. stores, ~1 per 52,000 population), parts availability, and the "Loan-a-Tool" program (unique, creates stickiness).
- **Do-Nothing Prevalence:** Estimated 20–30% of needed repairs are deferred (AAPEX/AAA data), representing latent demand that activates during economic pressure or vehicle failure events.

### DIFM/Commercial Customer Profile

**Analysis:**
- **Customer Type:** ~85% independent repair shops (IRFs), ~10% dealerships, ~5% fleet operators.
- **Pain Points:** Parts availability (right part, right time, within 30 minutes), credit terms, return policies, and technical support.
- **Mission-Criticality:** Extremely high — a shop with a bay occupied waiting for a part loses $50–100+ in labor revenue per hour.
- **Switching Costs (Higher in DIFM):** Higher than DIY due to relationship with commercial sales representatives, net-30 credit accounts, centralized billing, and delivery speed guarantees.
- **Procurement Blocker:** DIFM shops require guaranteed delivery times typically ≤ 30 minutes. AutoZone's commercial hub build-out (targeting same-day delivery from >90% of U.S. locations per management commentary) addresses this but lags ORLY's infrastructure.

### AutoZone's Loyalty Program

**Fact:** AutoZone's AutoZone Rewards program had approximately 37 million active members as of FY2024 (per management commentary, Q4 FY2024 earnings call). This represents a significant behavioral dataset for inventory optimization and targeted marketing.

## Section 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY

### Pricing Governance

**Fact/Analysis:** AutoZone maintains a proprietary pricing model that factors in:
- Competitor pricing (competitive price-matching policy in stores)
- Part category (failure parts command higher margins; discretionary accessories are more elastic)
- Brand tier (Duralast [house brand] vs. national brands like ACDelco, Bosch)

**Fact:** Duralast (AZO's private-label brand) represents an estimated **50%+ of domestic unit sales** (per management commentary and industry analyst estimates), providing both pricing flexibility and higher margins than branded alternatives.

### Elasticity Evidence

**Analysis:**
- **Failure parts (non-discretionary):** Price elasticity is low (estimated −0.2 to −0.4 based on academic work on necessity goods). A broken alternator must be replaced regardless of price ±$20.
- **Maintenance parts (semi-discretionary):** Moderate elasticity (estimated −0.5 to −0.8). Consumers may delay oil changes or wipers but not indefinitely.
- **Accessories:** Higher elasticity (estimated −1.0 to −1.5); clearly discretionary.

### Pricing Power Evidence

**Fact:** AutoZone successfully passed through inflationary cost increases in FY2022–2023, maintaining gross margins above 52% despite COGS inflation, demonstrating realized pricing power (per AZO 10-K FY2022, FY2023).

**Inference:** ARPU ceiling before churn inflects is estimated at approximately +15–20% above current prices for failure parts (given switching cost of store proximity and parts availability), and approximately +5–10% for maintenance/accessories before consumers shift to discount alternatives.

## Section 17: SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

### Supplier Landscape

**Fact/Analysis:**
- AutoZone sources from thousands of suppliers globally, with an estimated **15–25% sourced from China** (industry estimate; AZO does not disclose precise China sourcing percentage in filings).
- Key categories with China exposure: Electronic components, certain hard parts (alternators, starters from lower-cost manufacturers), tools and equipment, accessories.
- Duralast-branded parts are sourced from third-party manufacturers globally; AZO specifies the design and quality standards but does not manufacture internally.

### Tariff Risk (Current — June 2025)

**Fact:** The Trump administration's tariff regime (Section 301 + additional executive tariffs) as of mid-2025 affects Chinese-manufactured goods with rates ranging from 25% to 145% depending on category. Auto parts are in the 25–100%+ range depending on HTS classification.

**Analysis:**
- **Sourcing mix shift:** AZO and peers have been actively diversifying sourcing toward Taiwan, Vietnam, Mexico, and India since 2019.
- **Historical precedent (2018–2019):** During the first round of Section 301 tariffs, AZO's gross margin declined ~30 bps before passing through price increases, recovering within 18 months.
- **Current exposure estimate:** If 20% of COGS is China-sourced and tariffs add 40 percentage points to cost on those items, the gross COGS impact before sourcing diversification is approximately:
  - $17.46B revenue × 47% COGS = ~$8.2B COGS
  - × 20% China exposure = $1.64B affected
  - × 40% tariff rate = $655M annual cost headwind
  - This represents approximately **7.9% of COGS** or **370–380 bps of gross margin** before pass-through
  - With 60–70% pass-through and sourcing diversification reducing exposure to 10–15% within 12 months: **Net gross margin impact ~100–150 bps in FY2025–2026**

### Distribution Network

**Fact:** AZO operates approximately 100 distribution centers and hub stores domestically (per 10-K and management commentary). The mega-hub model (stores with 80,000–100,000 SKUs vs. standard stores with 20,000–25,000 SKUs) is expanding.

### Single Point of Failure

**Inference:** AZO's Reno, Nevada distribution center is a key western U.S. hub. A disruption (earthquake, fire, labor action) could impact supply to thousands of stores. However, AZO's distributed network reduces single-point-of-failure risk versus a more centralized model.
