### Switching Costs and Workflow Integration

**[Analysis]** Switching costs are segment-specific and significant:

- **Core Lab Diagnostics:** Hospitals sign 5–7 year reagent rental contracts with analyzers installed (capital equipment). Switching cost estimated at $500K–$2M per hospital in retraining, downtime, and validation. Gross retention rates typically >95%.
- **CGM:** Switching from FreeStyle Libre to Dexcom requires new sensor prescription, pharmacy prior authorization, and patient habit change. However, switching friction is lower than implied — Dexcom G7 and Libre 3 are both pharmacy-dispensed; the key lock-in is physician familiarity and app ecosystem.
- **Nutrition (Similac):** Consumer brand loyalty is moderate; WIC (Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children) contracts provide formulaic lock-in in the U.S. (~50% of formula market goes through WIC), but these are competitively bid every 3–5 years.
- **EPD:** Prescription relationships in emerging markets have moderate switching costs; Abbott's brand equity (e.g., Duphalac, Creon) is high in specific therapeutic areas.

### Procurement Blocker

**[Analysis]** The single biggest procurement blocker for CGM growth in the U.S. is **insurance prior authorization complexity** for non-insulin Type 2 patients. Physicians report 30–45 minute administrative burden per CGM authorization, limiting prescription rates. This is precisely why the CMS NCD ruling (Q3 2025) is a potential step-change catalyst — a positive ruling would create Medicare coverage by default for ~65% of the Type 2 non-insulin patient pool.

**[Fact]** In leadless pacing, Abbott's AVEIR competes directly with Medtronic's Micra AV. AVEIR DR (dual-chamber) is the first-to-market dual-chamber leadless pacemaker, providing a genuine competitive advantage with an estimated 12–18 month head start.

### Diagnostics

**[Fact]** Core laboratory diagnostics is dominated by Roche (~30% global share), Abbott (~14%), Siemens Healthineers (~13%), and Danaher/Beckman Coulter (~12%). Competition is primarily on reagent pricing, analyzer throughput, and service contract terms.

**[Analysis]** Win/loss in core lab is primarily driven by: (1) total cost of ownership (reagent prices, service), (2) analyzer throughput and turnaround time, and (3) menu breadth. Abbott's Alinity platform is competitive on all three dimensions but is not a clear market leader.

## Section 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

### Pricing Governance

**[Analysis]** Abbott's CGM pricing is governed by: (1) U.S. insurance reimbursement rates (Medicare DMEPOS — Durable Medical Equipment, Prosthetics, Orthotics and Supplies — fee schedule), (2) commercial payer negotiated rates (PBM formularies), and (3) OTC/self-pay pricing. Abbott has raised CGM sensor list prices approximately 5–8% annually since 2020, partially offset by rebates to PBMs.

### Elasticity Evidence

**[Fact]** The Lingo OTC CGM launch (U.S., UK; 2024) provides a real-world elasticity test: at $49/month, Abbott is targeting the wellness consumer segment (non-diabetic). Early sales data (Q3–Q4 2024) suggest modest traction (~200K users in first year, vs management's aspirational $500K target by end-2025). This suggests demand elasticity is meaningful — price matters in OTC CGM even among wellness consumers.

### ARPU Ceiling Before Churn Inflects

**[Inference]** Based on European FSL pricing (€60–70/month) and U.S. commercial pricing (~$130/month self-pay), the estimated ARPU ceiling before meaningful patient-initiated churn is approximately $150–160/month in the U.S. self-pay market. Above this level, patients shift to finger-stick monitoring (BGM), reducing CGM utilization. Current pricing is ~$85–130/month (depending on insurance vs self-pay), suggesting moderate headroom.
