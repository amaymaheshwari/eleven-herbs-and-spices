**[Analysis]** TFI does not operate a platform or ecosystem business in the traditional technology sense. This section addresses TFI's operational ecosystem — its network of owner-operators, acquired subsidiaries, and third-party partnerships.

### Owner-Operator Model (TL Segment)

**[Fact]** TFI employs a hybrid model: approximately 60–65% of its Truckload drivers are owner-operators (independent contractors) rather than company drivers. This model:
- Converts fixed costs (driver salaries, benefits) to variable costs
- Reduces capital intensity (no truck purchase/depreciation on owner-operator equipment)
- Creates asset-light margin profile in TL

**[Analysis]** The owner-operator model's risk is regulatory: the California Assembly Bill 5 (AB5) gig-economy classification law and potential federal independent contractor reclassification could force driver reclassification, adding USD 50–150M in annual costs (estimated, based on 5,000–10,000 affected drivers × USD 10–20K annual cost differential). This risk is real but partially mitigated by TFI's limited California exposure relative to peers.

### Subsidiary Network

**[Fact]** TFI has acquired approximately 500 operating companies since its founding. Each subsidiary operates with significant autonomy under TFI's holding structure, enabling entrepreneurial culture preservation while centralizing capital allocation.

**[Analysis]** Ecosystem health metric: subsidiary ROIC above cost of capital. TFI reports a "return on equity" metric implicitly; our estimate of weighted average subsidiary ROIC is ~12–14%, above our estimated WACC of 9–10%.
