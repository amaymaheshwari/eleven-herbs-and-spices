## SECTION 2: MARKET STRUCTURE AND SIZE

**[Fact]** The U.S. video game market generated approximately $57.2 billion in total consumer spend in 2024, according to the Entertainment Software Association (ESA) 2025 report. However, the breakdown is critical:

| Segment | 2024 Size (Est.) | YoY Growth | GME Addressable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital game sales | ~$42B | +8–10% | Minimal (no distribution) |
| Physical game software | ~$3–4B | -15 to -20% | Core GME market |

| Collectibles/merchandise | ~$2B | +5–8% | Growing GME focus |
| Mobile gaming | ~$25B (US only) | +5% | None |

**[Analysis]** GME's Total Addressable Market (TAM) is overwhelmingly concentrated in physical software and hardware — segments that are either structurally declining (software) or cyclical (hardware). The physical software market has declined from approximately $8.8B in 2015 to an estimated $3.5B in 2024 — a 60% contraction in nine years (NPD/Circana data, various annual reports).

**[Fact]** Physical software now represents less than 7% of total U.S. gaming spend (vs. ~50% a decade ago). Major publishers — Electronic Arts (EA), Activision Blizzard, Take-Two Interactive — have explicitly stated strategies to reduce physical SKU allocation. Sony and Microsoft have released disc-less console variants, accelerating this trend.

**[Analysis]** GME's Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) — physical retail in the categories it actually sells — is approximately $7–9B globally (physical software + hardware + accessories sold at retail, not direct). GME's revenue of $1.28B implies a market share of approximately 14–18% of this shrinking SAM. This share is not expanding; it is contracting in absolute terms as the SAM itself shrinks.

**Binding constraint:** Demand is the binding constraint. Physical game buyers are switching to digital permanently. There is no supply-side bottleneck that GME can solve; the issue is secular demand destruction.

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Analysis]** GME has essentially zero pricing power on new software and hardware — prices are set by publishers and platform holders. GameStop's price on a new AAA title is identical to Amazon's suggested retail price; any attempt to price above will result in immediate consumer migration.

**Pre-owned software** represents GME's only area of modest pricing discretion. However, eBay and Facebook Marketplace serve as continuous price anchors, limiting upward pricing moves. GME's buy prices (trade-in values) are notoriously low relative to market — this has been a persistent customer complaint and brand detractor.

**[Fact]** GME raised PowerUp Rewards Pro membership pricing from $14.99 to $19.99/year in 2023 (company announcements, various media). This suggests minimal subscription pricing power exists but the subscriber base is too small to matter financially.

**Willingness-to-pay ceiling:** For new games, the consumer's reference price is the digital store price. GME cannot charge above this without losing the sale. For pre-owned, GME competes with used prices on eBay — typically 10–20% below new price for recent titles, 60–90% below for older titles.

**ARPU ceiling before churn inflects:** Given digital substitution dynamics, the ARPU ceiling is effectively the digital download price — and GME cannot reach that ceiling because consumers can access digital directly.

**[Fact]** GME's supply chain is a traditional consumer electronics/entertainment retail supply chain:

**Key suppliers:**

**[Fact]** GME carries approximately $300–400M in inventory (balance sheet). Inventory management has improved as the company reduces ordering; pre-owned inventory is purchased from consumers (C2B) and doesn't carry supplier-dependency risk.

**Single point of failure:** GME's logistics and distribution is managed through a small number of distribution centers. Disruption here (natural disaster, labor action) would affect replenishment.
