## SECTION 1 — THESIS FRAMING

*[Analysis]* WD-40 Company is a single-product-dominant consumer/industrial brand masquerading as a diversified specialty-chemicals business. The core thesis is **not** about revenue growth acceleration but about **margin recovery, geographic penetration, and capital-return compounding** on a brand with near-zero private-label substitution risk in its core use case.

### Value-Creation Hurdle

*[Fact]* WD-40's FY2024 (ended August 31, 2024) revenue was $553.5 million, and diluted EPS was $5.50 (per 10-K filing, FY2024). The company targets a gross margin of 55% (stated long-range plan), versus the FY2024 realized gross margin of approximately 53.6%. Each 100 basis points of gross-margin expansion on the current revenue base generates approximately $5.5M incremental gross profit, or roughly $0.35 per diluted share after tax (assuming ~14.7M diluted shares and 24% effective tax rate).

### Customer Mix

*[Fact]* WD-40 sells through: (1) retail/DIY consumers (~60% of volume, per company disclosures), (2) industrial/professional tradespeople (~30%), and (3) OEM/industrial distributors (~10%). No single customer accounts for more than 10% of revenues (FY2024 10-K).

### Job-to-Be-Done Framework

| Segment | Primary Job | Mission-Criticality | Switching Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY Consumer | Lubricate stuck mechanisms, prevent rust | Low-moderate (convenience) | Very low — but brand habit is strong |
| Tradesperson/MRO | Rapid field fix, reliability guarantee | High (time = money) | Moderate — brand = trusted tool |
| Industrial distributor | Inventory predictability, consistent supply | High (supply chain continuity) | Moderate-high (logistics relationships) |
| OEM/Specialty | Specific formulation performance spec | High (engineering spec compliance) | High (re-qualification cost) |

*[Analysis]* The key insight is that switching cost for the **consumer segment is behaviorally rather than contractually high** — WD-40's ~60-year brand history and "blue-and-yellow can" recognition create habitual repurchase that mimics high switching cost even without formal contracts.

### Do-Nothing Prevalence

*[Inference]* In mature markets (Americas, EMEA), do-nothing (using alternative products or no product) is estimated at <10% of the addressable user base given high awareness. In APAC, do-nothing or local-substitute prevalence may be 40–60% of the addressable market.

### Procurement Blocker

*[Analysis]* In industrial/B2B channels, the key procurement blocker is **specification approval** — once WD-40 is written into a maintenance SOP or engineering spec, displacement requires a formal re-qualification process, estimated at 6–18 months for mid-sized manufacturers.

## SECTION 17 — SUPPLY CHAIN AND OPERATIONS

### Manufacturing and Supply Chain

*[Fact]* WD-40 operates a **primarily outsourced manufacturing model**. Key facts from FY2024 10-K:
- Products are manufactured at third-party contract manufacturers globally (US, UK, Australia, China, India, and others)
- The primary raw materials are petroleum-derived base oils, aerosol propellants (LPG/dimethyl ether), and steel cans
- The company owns the **formulation** but does not own most manufacturing capacity

### Critical Suppliers and Single-Source Exposures

| Input | Supplier Concentration | Single-Source Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petroleum-derived base oil | Multiple global suppliers | Low | Spot + term contracts |
| Aerosol propellant (LPG/DME) | Regional, moderate concentration | Moderate | 2–3 approved suppliers per region |
| Steel aerosol cans | 2–3 major suppliers per region | **Moderate-High** | Some dual-sourcing; long lead time on can tooling |
| Contract manufacturers | Multiple per region | **Moderate** (key US/UK plants) | Redundancy exists but switching takes 6–12 months |

### Input Cost Dynamics

*[Fact]* Petroleum-derivative costs (base oils, propellants) represent approximately 20–25% of the cost of goods sold (COGS) structure. The spike in these costs in FY2021–FY2022 compressed gross margin from ~55% to ~47%. The subsequent normalization has driven margin recovery back to ~53.6% in FY2024.

*[Analysis]* **WTI crude oil and LPG price are the primary external drivers of gross margin.** At current crude oil prices (~$75–80/bbl as of May 2025), the trajectory supports continued modest margin recovery. Each $10/bbl increase in crude reduces gross margin by approximately 50–80 bps (Inference, derived from COGS structure).

### Single Point of Failure

*[Inference]* The most significant single point of failure is **the UK manufacturing/distribution facility**, which serves as the primary production and fulfillment hub for EMEA. A major disruption (fire, labor action, Brexit-related logistics issues) could affect ~37% of revenue for 3–6 months.

## SECTION 19 — M&A STRATEGY AND OPTIONALITY

### Historical M&A

*[Fact]* WD-40 has been primarily an **organic-growth company**. Notable historical transactions:
- **FY2016:** Sold the Lava and Solvol heavy-duty hand cleaner brands to focus on core WD-40 portfolio
- **FY2018–present:** No material acquisitions; strategy has been organic expansion of Specialist line and geographic penetration

### Build-Buy-Partner Framework

*[Analysis]* Given the company's brand and distribution strength, the optimal M&A framework is:
- **Build:** APAC penetration and Specialist line extension — best done organically (brand integrity risk from acquisition)
- **Buy:** Adjacent specialty chemical/maintenance brand with complementary distribution (e.g., a professional tradespeople brand in EMEA or APAC) — logical but not yet visible in pipeline
- **Partner:** Co-distribution arrangements in frontier markets (Africa, Southeast Asia) — low-capital, appropriate for early-stage penetration

### Capability Gap That Cannot Be Built Organically

*[Inference]* The only capability gap that might require acquisition is **direct e-commerce/D2C infrastructure** in China (Tmall, JD.com integration) — this could be accelerated through a China-based distribution partnership or acquisition of a local MRO e-commerce player. Estimated deal size: $20–75M. Management has not signaled this publicly.

### M&A Risk

*[Analysis]* The risk of a **large, dilutive, off-strategy acquisition** is low given management's track record, but the clean balance sheet ($80M net debt, ~$80M annual FCF) provides the capacity for a $300–400M deal — large enough to be disruptive if poorly executed. This is a **tail risk, not a base case**.
