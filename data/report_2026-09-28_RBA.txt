### Thesis in One Paragraph

**Analysis.** RB Global is the dominant global industrial auction and marketplace operator, formed from the 2023 merger of Ritchie Bros. Auctioneers and IAA (Insurance Auto Auctions). The investment case rests on five pillars: (1) irreplaceable global auction network with 40+ years of buyer/seller data; (2) the IAA integration unlocking cross-sell and GMV (Gross Merchandise Volume) density in a structurally underpenetrated salvage-vehicle market; (3) a shift from fixed-price to marketplace/digital take-rate revenue that should expand margins structurally; (4) a debt paydown trajectory that re-rates equity as leverage normalizes; and (5) a management team (CEO Erik Jacobs, installed post-merger) with a credible operating playbook. The bear case centers on integration execution risk, elevated leverage (~3.5× Net Debt/EBITDA), competitive pressure from Copart and KAR/OPENLANE, and a valuation that, at current prices, does not offer the required margin of safety. ## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

### Pricing Governance

**Fact.** RB Global uses a **percentage-of-sale fee model** (both buyer and seller pay a percentage of the hammer price) in industrial auctions. IAA uses a hybrid: fixed per-unit fees + percentage components. Fee schedules are renegotiated annually for large insurance accounts; smaller industrial sellers face posted-rate cards.

### Elasticity Evidence

**Analysis.** In industrial auctions, RB tested buyer fee increases of ~50–100 bps in FY2022 with minimal buyer attrition, evidencing moderate pricing power (per RBA 2022 Annual Report commentary). In IAA, fee increases face resistance because Copart is a direct competitor with transparent pricing — elasticity is higher on the seller side.

### ARPU Ceiling Before Churn Inflects

**Inference.** Based on competitive analysis: industrial buyer fees >15% of ASP would likely trigger buyer substitution to Proxibid/online platforms. Seller fees >10% of ASP in industrial would risk losing large fleet consignors to direct dealer channels. Current blended take-rate of ~10.5–11% appears to have ~100–150 bps of headroom before churn inflects meaningfully.

### Reference Price Anchoring

**Analysis.** RB Global's reference price (the auction result) is publicly disclosed — this is a double-edged sword. It builds trust but also enables competitors to benchmark and undercut. Copart's closed-model prevents this reference-price anchoring, which is a structural advantage for Copart.
