### Growth Drivers (Quantified)

1. **Structural housing deficit:** The National Association of Realtors (NAR) estimated a cumulative housing shortage of 5.5–6.8 million units as of 2024 (Lawrence Yun, NAR, 2024 report). This provides multi-year demand tailwind.
2. **Millennial/Gen Z household formation:** ~4.4 million Americans turn 32–34 annually through 2027 — peak first-time homebuyer ages. (U.S. Census Bureau, Current Population Survey 2024.)
3. **Lock-in effect:** ~85% of outstanding mortgages carry rates below 5.0% (Federal Reserve Flow of Funds, Q4 2024), suppressing existing home supply and diverting buyers to new construction.
4. **Entry-level demand surge:** Median new home price declined ~6% from peak to ~$407,000 (April 2025, Census Bureau); DHI's Express Homes segment targets <$300K, addressing the most underserved cohort.

### Demand vs. Supply Constraint
**[Analysis]** Supply is the binding constraint. Lot availability, skilled labor, and permitting timelines limit production more than aggregate demand. DHI's 37,000-lot owned/optioned pipeline (FY2024 10-K) represents a strategic moat. However, affordability at current mortgage rates (~6.8–7.0% in early 2025) remains the demand-side binding constraint at the margin.

### TAM-Shrinkage Scenarios
- **Recession scenario:** New home sales fall to 450–500K/year (2008–2012 trough: 300–375K). At trough, SAM ~$220B; DHI SOM ~$130B.
- **Affordability cliff:** If rates rise to 8%+, SAM contracts 20–25% in volume terms.

### Sales Productivity
**[Fact]** DHI operated 8,650 active selling communities as of Q2 FY2025 (March 31, 2025) (Source: DHI Q2 FY2025 10-Q). Net new orders in Q2 FY2025 were 22,437 units, implying approximately 2.6 net orders per community per month — a standard measure of sales velocity.

**[Analysis]** 2.6 orders/community/month is below the historical peak of ~3.5 (FY2021 boom) but above the FY2022 trough (~1.8). Recovering to 3.0+ would signal meaningful demand re-acceleration.

### Biggest Funnel Bottleneck
**[Analysis]** The primary bottleneck is buyer mortgage qualification at current rates. DHI addresses this through forward commitments on mortgage rate buydowns (DHI Mortgage buys down buyer rates to 5.5–6.0% for 2–3 years), at a cost of approximately $15,000–$25,000 per unit. This buydown cost is embedded in cost of goods sold (COGS), suppressing reported gross margins but enabling volume.

### Doubling Pipeline Without Doubling OpEx
**[Inference]** Achieving this requires: (1) higher digital lead-to-close conversion (currently ~3–5%; industry best ~7–10%), (2) standardizing spec plans to reduce sales cycle, and (3) expanding DHI Mortgage pre-approval funnel. These levers are identified but execution pace is uncertain.

## SECTION 10: PRICING POWER AND ELASTICITY TESTING

**[Analysis]** DHI's pricing power is moderate and cyclical — it correlates closely with housing market conditions and mortgage rate levels.

### Pricing Governance
- Prices set community-by-community by regional division presidents
- Weekly pricing reviews based on sales velocity and competitive data
- Incentive/buydown structure managed centrally via DHI Mortgage

### Elasticity Evidence

**[Fact]** Average selling price (ASP) per closing:
- FY2022 peak: ~$444,000
- FY2023: ~$380,000 (−14%; driven by incentive deployment)
- FY2024: ~$387,000 (modest recovery)
- Q2 FY2025: ~$383,000 (modest decline)
(Source: DHI quarterly 10-Qs and 10-K filings.)

**[Analysis]** The ASP decline of ~14% from peak (with gross margins declining from 28% to ~22%) demonstrates meaningful price elasticity when mortgage rates rise. The company chose volume defense over price defense — a deliberate strategic choice consistent with its market-share focus.

### Mortgage Buydown Economics (Pricing Power Mechanism)
**[Calculation]** At Q2 FY2025:
- Estimated buydown cost: ~$20,000/unit (management commentary)
- Closings: ~22,000/quarter
- Total quarterly buydown cost: ~$440M embedded in COGS
- If mortgage rates fall 100 bps, buydown cost declines ~50% → ~$220M quarterly gross profit recapture
- Gross margin impact: ~$220M / ~$8.6B quarterly homebuilding revenue = ~+250 bps gross margin tailwind

**[Inference]** Mortgage rate normalization to 6.0–6.5% could restore 150–250 bps of gross margin without changing any other variable — a key option value embedded in the business.

### ARPU Ceiling Before Volume Inflects
**[Analysis]** Based on NAHB affordability data, the maximum sustainable ASP for entry-level (Express) buyers at current income levels (~$80K household) and 7% mortgage rate is approximately $290,000 (assumes 28% front-end debt-to-income (DTI), 10% down). DHI's Express ASP of ~$275–310K is near this ceiling, limiting further price increase absent income growth or rate reduction.
